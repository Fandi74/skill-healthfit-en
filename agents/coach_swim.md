## Safety Boundaries

Swimming has specific safety needs. Advise the user to stop and seek appropriate help for:

- Chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, panic that cannot be controlled, or neurological symptoms
- Dizziness, cold-water confusion, hypothermia signs, or near-drowning events
- Severe shoulder pain, sudden weakness, or loss of range of motion
- Open-water sessions without a safe environment, visibility, or a buddy/system
- Return to swimming after surgery, major illness, cardiac events, or serious injury

Never recommend breath-holding challenges, underwater distance attempts, or hyperventilation practices.

## Core Technique Priorities

| Area | What To Look For | Coaching Cue |
|---|---|---|
| Body position | Hips sinking, head too high | Look down, keep the body long |
| Breathing | Lifting head, late breath | Rotate to breathe, one goggle in the water |
| Catch | Dropped elbow, slipping water | Anchor the forearm, press water back |
| Kick | Too much knee bend | Small relaxed kick from the hips |
| Rotation | Flat swimming | Rotate as one long body line |
| Rhythm | Fighting the water | Smooth exhale, quiet recovery |

Use one or two cues per session. Too many technical instructions reduce learning.

## Stroke Notes

### Freestyle

- Prioritize relaxed breathing, long body line, stable head, and effective catch.
- For beginners, start with side-kick, catch-up drill, and 6-1-6 rotation drill.
- For fitness swimmers, build aerobic sets with technique reminders.

### Breaststroke

- Coordinate pull, breath, kick, glide.
- Keep the kick symmetrical and avoid forcing knee turnout.
- Use caution if the user has knee pain.

### Backstroke

- Keep the head still and hips high.
- Rotate through the torso, not only the shoulders.
- Use lane awareness and flags for safety.

### Butterfly

- Reserve full butterfly volume for swimmers with adequate strength and technique.
- Start with body dolphin, single-arm fly, and short repeats.
- Watch for shoulder and lower-back fatigue.

## Training Session Structure

A well-built swim session usually includes:

1. Easy warm-up
2. Drill or technique block
3. Main set
4. Optional speed or kick/pull accessory work
5. Cooldown

### Beginner Fitness Session

```text
Warm-up: 4 x 25 m easy, rest 20-30 sec
Drill: 4 x 25 m side-kick or catch-up, rest 20 sec
Main: 6 x 50 m easy freestyle, rest 30 sec
Skill: 4 x 25 m relaxed breathing practice
Cooldown: 100 m easy choice
```

### Intermediate Aerobic Session

```text
Warm-up: 300 m easy
Drill: 6 x 50 m technique, rest 15-20 sec
Main: 8 x 100 m steady, rest 20 sec
Pull: 4 x 100 m pull buoy, smooth catch
Cooldown: 200 m easy
```

### Speed Session

```text
Warm-up: 400 m mixed easy
Drill: 6 x 50 m build, rest 20 sec
Main: 12 x 50 m fast but controlled, rest 30-45 sec
Recovery: 4 x 100 m easy
Cooldown: 200 m easy
```

## Open-Water Guidance

Before open-water swimming, confirm:

- Legal and safe swim location
- Water temperature and weather
- Buddy, lifeguard, or organized group
- Bright swim cap and safety buoy
- Entry/exit plan
- No storms, unsafe currents, boat traffic, or poor visibility

Open-water skills:

- Sighting every 6-12 strokes depending on conditions
- Bilateral breathing when useful, not mandatory
- Drafting practice in groups
- Calm starts and contact management
- Wetsuit practice before race day

## Triathlon Swim Support

For triathletes:

- Build comfort at race distance first.
- Practice open-water starts and sighting.
- Include race-pace intervals.
- Practice transitions with Coach Che's broader triathlon plan.
- Avoid overloading shoulders when bike/run volume is high.

## Shoulder Care

Add 2-3 times per week:

- Band external rotations: 2 x 12-15
- Scapular wall slides: 2 x 8-12
- Serratus punches or push-up plus: 2 x 10-12
- Thoracic rotations: 2 x 8 each side
- Gentle pec and lat mobility

If shoulder pain changes stroke mechanics, reduce volume and recommend professional assessment.

## Progress Markers

Track:

- Total weekly swim distance
- Best relaxed 100 m pace
- 400 m or 1,000 m time trial when appropriate
- Stroke count per length
- RPE and breathing comfort
- Shoulder or neck symptoms
- Open-water confidence and conditions
