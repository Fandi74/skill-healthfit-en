# HealthFit TCM Glossary
# Format: #ID | Term | Pinyin | Definition | Related Constitution/Application

#101 | Qi Deficiency | qì xū | Insufficient original qi, manifested as fatigue and lack of strength, shortness of breath and reluctance to speak, easy sweating, and catching colds easily | Qi-deficiency constitution

#102 | Yang Deficiency | yáng xū | Insufficient yang qi, manifested as aversion to cold and fear of cold, cold hands and feet, preference for hot drinks, and loose stools | Yang-deficiency constitution

#103 | Yin Deficiency | yīn xū | Insufficient yin fluids, manifested as dry mouth and throat, heat in the palms and soles, night sweats, and constipation | Yin-deficiency constitution

#104 | Phlegm-Damp | tán shī | Pathological products formed by impaired fluid metabolism, manifested as heavy body sensation, abdominal fullness and obesity, and much phlegm | Phlegm-damp constitution

#105 | Damp-Heat | shī rè | Damp pathogen combined with heat pathogen, manifested as bitter and dry mouth, heavy body and fatigue, yellow urine, and sticky stools | Damp-heat constitution

#106 | Blood Stasis | xuè yū | Poor blood circulation or extravasated blood stagnating outside the channels, manifested as dull skin color, purplish-dark lips, and easy spot formation | Blood-stasis constitution

#107 | Qi Constraint | qì yù | Stagnation and poor flow of qi dynamics, manifested as depressed mood, frequent sighing, and foreign-body sensation in the throat | Qi-constraint constitution

#108 | Balanced Constitution | píng hé zhì | An ideal constitutional state in which yin, yang, qi, and blood are harmonized; abundant energy, good sleep, and cheerful personality | One of the nine constitutions

#109 | Special Diathesis Constitution | tè bǐng zhì | Abnormal congenital endowment or allergic constitution, prone to allergies to medicines, foods, pollen, etc. | One of the nine constitutions

#110 | Tongue Image | shé xiàng | The shape, color, coating texture, etc. of the tongue; an important part of TCM inspection diagnosis | Dr. Chen

#111 | Tongue Body | shé tǐ | The shape and size of the tongue; normally pale red and moist, enlarged indicates qi deficiency/yang deficiency, thin and small indicates yin deficiency | Dr. Chen

#112 | Tongue Coating | shé tāi | The covering on the tongue surface; white coating indicates cold/exterior pattern, yellow coating indicates heat pattern, greasy coating indicates phlegm-damp | Dr. Chen

#113 | Teeth Marks | chǐ hén | Tooth pressure marks on the tongue edges, commonly seen in qi-deficiency, yang-deficiency, and phlegm-damp constitutions | Dr. Chen

#114 | Tonify Qi | bǔ qì | A treatment method for supplementing original qi; commonly used medicines and foods include astragalus, codonopsis, Chinese yam, and red dates | Regulation of qi-deficiency constitution

#115 | Warm Yang | wēn yáng | A treatment method for warming and tonifying yang qi; commonly used medicines and foods include cinnamon bark, dried ginger, lamb, and walnuts | Regulation of yang-deficiency constitution

#116 | Nourish Yin | zī yīn | A treatment method for nourishing yin fluids; commonly used medicines and foods include lily bulb, snow fungus, duck meat, and black sesame | Regulation of yin-deficiency constitution

#117 | Transform Phlegm and Dispel Dampness | huà tán qū shī | A treatment method for eliminating phlegm-dampness; commonly used medicines and foods include coix seed, poria, tangerine peel, and winter melon | Regulation of phlegm-damp constitution

#118 | Solar-Term Health Preservation | jié qì yǎng shēng | A health-preservation method that adjusts daily routine and diet according to the characteristics of the twenty-four solar terms | Dr. Chen

#119 | Sanfu | sān fú | The three hottest periods of summer, suitable for "treating winter diseases in summer"; moxibustion and herbal patches work best | Solar-term health preservation

#120 | Winter Solstice | dōng zhì | The time of the year when yin reaches its extreme and yang begins to arise; the best season for tonifying the kidneys and nourishing yang | Solar-term health preservation
